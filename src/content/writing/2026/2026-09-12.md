title: Communication
---
Being able to communicate thoughts, questions, concerns, etc. to people is a seriously underrated skill. Yes, everyone tells you to work on these, but from my experience it is difficult to do in practice because 1) most classes used to develop these skills (e.g. college communications classes) are pretty easy and 2) a lot of people tend to spend most of their time in their comfort zone and spend the moajority of their to other people with similar backgrounds and ways of communication to them (not a blanket statement! It just feels this way). I think a great way to practice these skills is to simply do it and watch other people (that you deem have some credibility) do it. For example, listen to an interview by Andrew Ross Sorkin on Squawk Box (every morning!) and observe how he probes guests, understands what they want and don't want to say, and continues probing them to ultimately draw a series of conclusion(s) about what he originally wanted to cover. After observing others do it, distill what they did right (in your opinion, of course) and what they did wrong, and take into account what they did right in your skillset. Then practice these things during work, interviews, meeting new people, etc.

This system might not work for everyone, they are just something that I have come to develop over time and feel that they have helped me.
